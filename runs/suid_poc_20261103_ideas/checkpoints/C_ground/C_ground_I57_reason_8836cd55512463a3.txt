### Design

**Design:** Trends within cases. Compare proportions among the cases across periods.

**Restated Question:**
How does the trend of SUID cases with previous child deaths compare over time to trends in infant care arrangements?

**Comparison Periods:**
- Case series (ncfrp_v51): 2008-2019
- Population reference (prams_p7_arf): 2012-2015

### Sources by Role

**Case Series:** ncfrp_v51
- **Role:** Records SUID cases and their history of previous child deaths.
- **Coverage:** SUID cases reviewed by fatality review teams in participating states from 2008 to 2019.

**Population Reference:** prams_p7_arf
- **Role:** Provides survey data on infant care arrangements among live births.
- **Coverage:** Weighted survey of mothers a few months after live births, sampled from birth certificates in participating states for the years 2012 through 2015. 

**Denominator Source:**
- Not explicitly needed since this study is comparing trends within case series (ncfrp_v51) and population reference (prams_p7_arf).

### Period

**Years Needed:**
- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51: 2008-2019
- **Population Reference:** prams_p7_arf: 2012-2015

The years held now are sufficient for both the case series and population reference.

### Variables

**Concepts to Measure:**
1. **Previous Child Deaths (Case Series):**
   - ncfrp_v51: 
     - SUPdthneglct, GIV1dthneglct, BIO1dthneglct, GIV2dthneglct
     - SUPdthabuse, BIO1dthabuse, BIO2dthneglct
     - SUPdthsids, BIO1dthsids, GIV1dthsids
     - RES1dthneglct, SUPdthother, GIV2priordeath, GIV1dthsuicide, GIV1dthaccid, GIV1dthundeter, GIV1dthunk

   **Notes:** These variables capture different types of prior child deaths (e.g., neglect, abuse, SIDS). Each variable is coded as "yes" or "no."

2. **Infant Care Arrangements (Population Reference):**
   - prams_p7_arf:
     - INFWMOM5: Infant living with mom (FORCED SKIP)
     - VST_CARE_RAW: Health worker visit to help care for self/baby
     - INF_ICU: Infant ICU -- at birth

**Coding of Missing Answers:**
- **Case Series:** 
  - Unknown responses are documented per item.
  
- **Population Reference:** 
  - Don't know or skipped items are coded separately (e.g., .B = don’t know or blank, .S = skipped).

### Biases

#### Selection Bias:
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** Cases are reviewed by fatality review teams in participating states. Participation varies over time, and the number of cases reflects changes in participation rather than true incidence.
  
- **Population Reference (prams_p7_arf):** Mothers surveyed after live births represent a sample from birth certificates; these surveys do not include infant deaths.

#### Information Bias:
- **Case Series:** Reporting flags indicate missing or unreported information. The data on previous child deaths may be incomplete due to reporting practices.
  
- **Population Reference:** Survey items are reported retrospectively, and missing values (don't know, skipped) need careful handling when calculating prevalence.

#### Confounding:
- Potential confounders include maternal smoking, prenatal care visits, socio-economic status, urban/rural setting, etc., which could affect both previous child deaths and infant care arrangements. These variables should be considered in analyses to mitigate confounding effects.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

1. **Incidence:** The study design does not provide denominators to estimate incidence or rates.
2. **Causation:** Group-level comparisons cannot establish causality; only associations can be inferred.
3. **Fine-Grained Mechanisms:** Detailed mechanisms underlying SUID cannot be explored through these data.

### Prior Art

**Closest Related Claims:**
- The manuscript claims (e.g., C1, C8, C24) discuss trends and associations in SUID incidence but do not address the specific comparison between previous child deaths and infant care arrangements.
  
**Why Open:**
- No prior study has explored the correlation between SUID cases with a history of previous child deaths and infant care arrangements. This gap remains unaddressed.

### Verdict

These sources can answer a version of the question regarding trends in proportions of SUID cases with previous child deaths compared to trends in infant care arrangements within the specified periods (2008-2019 for case series; 2012-2015 for population reference). The study design supports group-level comparisons but cannot estimate incidence or establish causation.

**Conclusion:** Yes, these sources can answer a version of the question.
