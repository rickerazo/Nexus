### Design

**Design:** Case-population comparison. Compare how common an exposure is among the cases (from a case series) with its weighted prevalence in the general population (from a population reference source).

**Restated Question:**
Is the use of medications during preconception and pregnancy linked to SUID incidence? This study will compare the proportion of medication use among SUID cases from the NCFRP_v51 or CDC_linkco15 case series with the weighted prevalence of similar medication exposure in a population reference source (PRAMS_p7_arf and PRAMS_p8_arf).

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:**
  - **CDC_linkco15:** Provides detailed information on infant deaths linked to birth records. It includes cases from US live births in the 2015 cohort, with their first-year outcomes.
  - **NCFRP_v51:** Provides reviewed child death cases by fatality review teams (SUID cases), although it does not provide incidence denominators.

- **Population Reference:**
  - **PRAMS_p7_arf:** Weighted survey of mothers a few months after live births in participating states, covering the years 2012-2015.
  - **PRAMS_p8_arf:** Similar to PRAMS_p7_arf but for the Phase 8 period (2016 onwards).

### Period

**Period:**
The proposed study covers the years 2012-2021. To ensure that all necessary data is available, we need:
- **CDC_linkco15:** Requires additional years beyond 2015 for a comprehensive analysis.
- **NCFRP_v51:** Covers 2008-2019 but needs more recent years (2020-2021).
- **PRAMS_p7_arf:** Already covers 2012-2015, so no additional data is needed.
- **PRAMS_p8_arf:** Covers 2016 onwards up to 2021.

### Variables

**Medication Use During Preconception and Pregnancy:**
- **Case Series (CDC_linkco15):**
  - Relevant variables like `F_CIGS_0` for cigarettes before pregnancy.
  
- **Case Series (NCFRP_v51):**
  - `INF3meds`: Did mother use any medications, drugs, or other substances during pregnancy?
  - Additional detailed medication variables (`IEMmedsarthritis`, `IEMmedspod`, etc.)

- **Population Reference (PRAMS_p7_arf and PRAMS_p8_arf):**
  - `PRE_RX`: Pre-pregnancy prescription medications.
  - Variables for specific conditions like asthma, allergies, nausea/vomiting, depression/anxiety, epilepsy, kidney/bladder infections.

### Biases

**Selection Bias:**
- **Case Series (CDC_linkco15 and NCFRP_v51):** Both sources include only SUID cases and may not be representative of the general population.
- **Population Reference (PRAMS_p7_arf and PRAMS_p8_arf):** Sampled mothers from live births, which excludes those who did not survive to birth.

**Information Bias:**
- Reporting flags in CDC_linkco15 and NCFRP_v51 may lead to underreporting or misclassification of medications.
- Timing and method of data collection differ between case series and population references (e.g., PRAMS collects data a few months post-birth, whereas NCFRP records are reviewed retrospectively).

**Confounding:**
- Confounders such as socioeconomic status, maternal age, education level, smoking during pregnancy, and other health conditions need to be accounted for.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

This design can only establish associations between medication use and SUID incidence. It cannot definitively prove causation due to observational nature of the data.

### Prior Art

- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10:** Discusses limitations related to missing maternal smoking status.
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C23:** Addresses potential confounding factors like prematurity and bed sharing.
- **suid_literature_abstracts.C11, C45:** Consistently reports on the association of maternal smoking with SUID risk.

The proposed study is distinct from these claims as it focuses specifically on medication use during preconception and pregnancy. 

### Verdict

**Can these sources answer a version of the question?**

Yes, the data can support an analysis to compare the proportion of medication use among SUID cases (from NCFRP_v51 or CDC_linkco15) with the weighted prevalence in PRAMS_p7_arf and PRAMS_p8_arf. The key limitations are the need for additional years beyond 2015 from CDC_linkco15 and the potential biases mentioned.

**Logical Reasoning:**
- Case series sources provide detailed information on SUID cases.
- Population reference sources provide weighted survey data on medication use among live births.
- Variables exist to measure preconception and pregnancy medication use in both types of sources, allowing for a group-level comparison.
