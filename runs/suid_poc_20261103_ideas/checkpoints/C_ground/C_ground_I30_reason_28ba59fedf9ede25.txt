### Design

**Question Restated:** Does inadequate postpartum health care access contribute to higher rates of Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID) in infants compared to non-SUID infant deaths?

The design involves a case-case comparison where cases of SUID are compared with other infant deaths recorded within the same dataset. This approach aims to identify factors that differentiate SUID cases from other causes of infant death, focusing on postpartum health care access as the primary exposure.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:** `ncfrp_v51` (National Fatality Review Case Reporting System) records child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams.
  - Reasons: It provides detailed information on individual cases of SUID and other infant deaths, which are necessary for case-case comparisons. However, it cannot provide incidence denominators or serve as a denominator source.

- **Population Reference:** `prams_p8_arf` (PRAMS Phase 8 Analytic Research File) surveys mothers after live births.
  - Reasons: It provides information on postpartum healthcare access from the general population reference, allowing for comparisons between SUID and non-SUID cases. However, it does not include infant death data.

### Period

The common period that lies within the coverage of both `ncfrp_v51` (2008-2019) and `prams_p8_arf` (2016-2021) is 2016-2019. This period is used for analysis.

### Variables

#### Postpartum Health Care Access
- **ncfrp_v51:INF4heduchospvis** - If yes, health education -- Hosp Stay -- Importance of postpartum visits.
- **prams_p8_arf:PPV_CHK** - PPV -- checkup for self (Postpartum Visit Check).

#### SUID Cases and Non-SUID Infant Deaths
- **ncfrp_v51:CaseImport** - Indicates if the case was imported from vital statistics.
- **ncfrp_v51:INFisscare** - Access or compliance issues related to prenatal care.

### Biases

#### Selection Bias:
- The `ncfrp_v51` dataset is incomplete as it only covers cases reviewed by participating fatality review teams, which may not be representative of all SUID and non-SUID infant deaths in the US.
- The PRAMS survey (`prams_p8_arf`) is a population-based reference but does not include information on infant deaths.

#### Information Bias:
- Differences in reporting between `ncfrp_v51` and `prams_p8_arf`: For example, postpartum health care access may be reported differently by mothers surveyed through PRAMS compared to the detailed case reviews conducted for SUID cases.
- Potential misclassification of exposures: Mothers' self-reported postpartum healthcare visits might differ from actual healthcare utilization.

#### Confounding Bias:
- Differences in demographic factors such as socioeconomic status, urban/rural location, and maternal health conditions (e.g., smoking) could confound the association between inadequate postpartum care access and SUID risk. These factors are not uniformly measured or available across both datasets.
  
### What the Design Cannot Establish

The design cannot establish causation due to its observational nature. It can only identify associations that may suggest a relationship but does not account for all potential confounders. Additionally, without incidence denominators, it cannot calculate rates or risks.

### Prior Art

- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10 [limitation]:** The analysis excludes postnatal exposures such as postpartum healthcare access.
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C24 [limitation]:** The case series should be understood as a surveillance sample rather than a fixed national census, limiting generalizability.

These limitations highlight that the influence of postpartum health care access on SUID rates is understudied and requires further investigation.

### Verdict

The proposed design can conduct a case-case comparison to identify differences in postpartum healthcare access between SUID cases and other infant deaths. However, it cannot establish causation or incidence due to the lack of denominator data. The study would need additional data on population-level exposure measures (e.g., actual rates of postpartum care utilization) to fully assess the risk associated with inadequate postpartum health care access.
