### Design

**Question Restated:** What is the incidence of Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID) among infants born to mothers who experienced significant medical complications during pregnancy compared to those with uncomplicated pregnancies, from 2016-2021?

**Design Type:**
Exposure-specific rates. Estimate outcome rates by exposure group by combining cases from a case series (numerator), the exposure prevalence from a population reference source, and the population at risk from a denominator source.

### Sources by Role

**Case Series:** ncfrp_v51
- **Role:** Provides SUID cases with information on maternal medical complications.
  
**Population Reference:** prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf
- **Role:** Supplies exposure prevalence for significant medical complications during pregnancy (weighted survey data from mothers of live births).

**Denominator Source:** cdc_natl15, cdc_linkco15
- **Role:** Provides population at risk (live births and denominators) to estimate incidence rates.

### Period

Years needed: 2016-2021

Years acquired:
- ncfrp_v51: Death data needs to be expanded to include cases from 2016-2021.
- prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf: Survey data for live births in 2016-2021 are already included (2016-2021).
- cdc_natl15, cdc_linkco15: Birth data from 2016-2021 can be acquired.

### Variables

**Maternal Medical Complications:**
- **ncfrp_v51:** INFmedical (During pregnancy did mother have medical complications/infections)
- **prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf:** MM_FEVER (BC: mom had fever), PG8_UTI (Health prob during preg -- Kidney/bladder infection), PG8_NAUS (Health prob during preg -- Nausea/vomiting/dehydration)

**Exposure Prevalence:**
- **prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf:** WTANAL (BC WT: ANALYSIS WT) for weighted survey data.

**Population at Risk:**
- **cdc_natl15, cdc_linkco15:** Birth records from 2016 to 2021.

### Biases

**Selection Bias:**
- ncfrp_v51 only includes cases reviewed by fatality review teams; it does not capture all SUID cases.
  
**Information Bias:**
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** Data on medical complications may be retrospectively reported and less reliable than contemporaneous data from live births.
- **Population Reference (PRAMS):** Self-reported data can suffer from recall bias or social desirability bias.

**Confounding:**
- Potential confounders like maternal smoking, socioeconomic status, and preterm birth are not consistently measured across all sources. 
- Lack of detailed medical records may lead to unmeasured confounders affecting the outcome (SUID).

### What the Design Cannot Establish

This design can estimate exposure-specific rates but cannot establish causation or temporal relationships due to its observational nature.

### Prior Art

**Manuscript Claims:**
- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10 [limitation]** and **bedsharing_ms_v7.C26 [limitation]** highlight limitations related to data quality and completeness, similar concerns apply here.
  
The closest prior work is:
- **suid_literature_abstracts.C10 [finding]**: Maternal obesity as a risk factor for SUID. This claim addresses another aspect of maternal health but does not address medical complications during pregnancy.

### Verdict

**Can Answer the Question:** Yes, with some limitations and necessary data acquisition.
- The design can estimate incidence rates using exposure-specific groups from 2016 to 2021 by combining case series (SUID cases), population reference (PRAMS survey data for medical complications), and denominator sources (CDC birth records).
- However, the study will need additional years of SUID case data (ncfrp_v51) from 2016-2021 to cover the full period.

**Limitations:**
- Selection bias due to non-comprehensive case series.
- Information biases in retrospective reporting.
- Confounding variables like maternal smoking and socioeconomic status may affect results without proper adjustment.
