### Design

**Question Restated:**
Does the presence of a hospital overnight stay impact SUID risk? This will be studied through a case-only analysis by comparing cases with and without hospital stays, examining potential differences in demographic and clinical characteristics.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51 (National Fatality Review Case Reporting System)
  - Covers: Child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams.
  - Includes information on SUID cases but does not provide incidence denominators for live births.
  
No denominator or population reference sources are needed since this is a case-only analysis focusing only on the characteristics of the cases with and without hospital stays.

### Period

**Period:** The manuscript focuses on data from 2008-2019. Given that ncfrp_v51 covers deaths from 2004 to 2019, we can use this period for our analysis.

### Variables

#### Hospital Overnight Stay
- **ncfrp_v51:INF4hosprtn** (Infant return to hospital overnight after delivery)
  - Value: 1 = Yes
  - Missing: Unknown is a separate answer code.

### Biases

**Selection Bias:** 
- The ncfrp_v51 source includes only cases reviewed by fatality review teams, which may not represent all SUID cases in the population.
  
**Information Bias:**
- Data on hospital stays are reported by reviewers and may vary based on reporting practices and availability of information.

**Confounding:**
- Other factors such as socioeconomic status, maternal health conditions (e.g., smoking), and birth outcomes (e.g., prematurity) could confound the association between hospital stays and SUID risk.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

The design can only establish associations among cases and cannot estimate incidence or risk differences since it lacks a denominator source. It also does not account for potential biases due to selection, information, and confounding factors.

### Prior Art

- **Manuscript Claims:**
  - bedsharing_ms_v7.C1 (hypothesis): Postconceptional age may clarify how various exposures relate to SUID incidence.
  - bedsharing_ms_v7.C3 (finding): Bed sharing was more strongly associated with SUID in an early developmental window compared to a later one.
  - bedsharing_ms_v7.C8 (finding): Bed sharing was recorded for 59.5% of SUID cases, and these cases were often associated with maternal smoking during pregnancy.

These claims focus on the timing of exposure effects but do not address hospital stays or their impact on SUID risk specifically.

### Verdict

**Can these data answer a version of the question?**

Yes, these data can be used to compare cases that experienced overnight hospital stays versus those who did not. However, due to the nature of the case-only design and lack of denominator data:
- **Limitation:** The analysis will only provide an association among SUID cases but cannot establish causality or estimate incidence rates.
- **Next Steps:** To further understand the impact of hospital stays on SUID risk, future studies should include a population reference to derive adjusted odds ratios and consider confounding factors.

### Summary

The ncfrp_v51 dataset can be used for a case-only analysis comparing cases with and without overnight hospital stays. However, additional data sources would be needed to estimate incidence rates or account for potential biases comprehensively.
