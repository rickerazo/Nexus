### Design

**Question Restated:** How does the prevalence of hospital overnight stays among SUID cases compare with that in live births from PRAMS Phase 8?

This is a case-population comparison study. It compares an exposure (hospital overnight stay) between two groups: 
- **Cases:** SUID cases from the National Fatality Review Case Reporting System (NFR-CRS)
- **Population Reference Group:** Live births from the Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System (PRAMS Phase 8)

### Sources by Role

**Case Series Source:**
- **ncfrp_v51:** The NFR-CRS codebook is used to identify SUID cases. It provides detailed case information but does not supply a denominator for incidence rates.

**Population Reference Source:**
- **prams_p8_arf:** PRAMS Phase 8 Analytic Research File (ARF) acts as the population reference source, providing survey data on live births from participating states to estimate exposure prevalence in the general population of live births. It includes weighted survey responses that can be used to calculate adjusted prevalences.

### Period

**Period Common to All Sources:**
- **PRAMS Phase 8 (2016-2021)**
- The NFR-CRS has data from 2004-2019, but the manuscript uses cases from 2008-2019.
- Therefore, the common period is 2016-2019.

### Variables

**Concept:** Hospital overnight stays after birth
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):**
  - Variable: `INF4hosprtn` – If yes, hospital overnight stay; how old was infant in weeks.
  - Missing answers are coded separately and should not be counted as exposed.

**Concept:** Live births with hospital stays
- **Population Reference (PRAMS p8_arf):**
  - Variable: `LTH_HOSP` – Length of time baby spent in the hospital.
  - Missing values (‘.B’ for don't know, ‘.S’ for skipped) should not be counted as exposed.

### Biases

**Selection Bias:**
- **Case Series:** The NFR-CRS data covers child deaths reviewed by participating fatality review teams; changes in case counts over time reflect participation rather than incidence.
- **Population Reference:** PRAMS Phase 8 samples mothers from live births, which may not be representative of all live births due to sampling and non-response biases.

**Information Bias:**
- The timing and method of data collection differ between the sources. NFR-CRS captures detailed case information retrospectively, while PRAMS collects survey responses from new mothers a few months after birth.
  
**Confounding:**
- Confounders such as maternal smoking, prematurity, NICU stays, preterm births, and socio-economic factors may influence both hospital overnight stays and SUID risk but are not directly measured in this comparison.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

This design can establish whether there is a difference in the prevalence of hospital overnight stays between SUID cases and live births. However, it cannot:
- Estimate incidence rates or risks.
- Determine causality; an association does not imply that hospital stays cause SUID.
- Control for potential confounders without additional data on these factors.

### Prior Art

The closest manuscript claims do not directly address the prevalence of hospital overnight stays in relation to SUID cases. Claims such as:
- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10:** The analysis excludes some extremely preterm infants who died early, which may be relevant but does not specifically discuss hospital stays.
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C8:** Indicates that bed sharing was more frequent among SUID cases and associated with maternal smoking.

These claims suggest that certain exposures (e.g., bed sharing) are more common in SUID cases compared to the general population, but they do not cover hospital overnight stays specifically.

### Verdict

**Can these data answer a version of the question?**
Yes, the data can compare the prevalence of hospital overnight stays among SUID cases with live births from PRAMS Phase 8. However, the study would be limited in its ability to control for confounders and establish causality without additional information on potential risk factors.

**Data Element or Design Element Missing:**
- **Additional Data:** To better understand the relationship between hospital overnight stays and SUID, data on other relevant exposures (e.g., maternal smoking, prematurity) would be necessary.
- **Design Improvement:** Including a denominator source to estimate incidence rates or risks could strengthen the study design.

In summary, while these datasets can establish an association between hospital overnight stays and SUID cases compared to live births, they cannot provide a complete causal inference without further data on potential confounders.
