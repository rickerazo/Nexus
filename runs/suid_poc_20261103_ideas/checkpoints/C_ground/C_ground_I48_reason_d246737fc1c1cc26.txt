### Study Design and Feasibility Evaluation

#### 1. The Design
**Restated Question:** How does maternal weight gain during pregnancy vary by maternal BMI among SUID cases?  
This is a case-only analysis comparing proportions or odds of different levels of pre-pregnancy BMI and weight gain within the case series.

#### 2. Sources by Role
- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51 (National Fatality Review Case Reporting System)  
  *Reason:* This dataset records child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams, focusing on SUID cases.
  
- **Population Reference:** prams_p8_arf and prams_p7_arf (PRAMS Phase 8 and Phase 7 Analytic Research Files)
  *Reason:* These datasets provide a population-level reference for maternal weight gain and pre-pregnancy BMI among live births.

- **Denominator Source:** None required, since this is a case-only study focusing on proportions within the case series.

#### 3. Period
**Period:** The manuscript uses data from 2008 to 2019 (ncfrp_v51) and PRAMS Phase 8 covers 2016-2021, but we must use overlapping years:  
*Final period:* 2016-2019

#### 4. Variables
**Maternal Weight Gain during Pregnancy:**  
- **ncfrp_v51:** INF4momwtgn (Mother's pregnancy weight gain or loss in pounds)
  - Missing answers: UNK = unknown, .B = don't know
- **PRAMS Phase 8 & Phase 7:** MOMLBS (Maternal Weight Gain (LBS))  
  - Missing answers: U = unknown

**Pre-pregnancy BMI:**
- **ncfrp_v51:** IEMprenatalBMI (Maternal prenatal BMI)
  - Missing answers: UNK = unknown
- **PRAMS Phase 8 & Phase 7:** MOM_BMIG_QX_REV (VAR: MOM BMI GROUPED FROM QX REVISED)  
  - Missing answers: .B = don't know, blank = unknown

#### 5. Biases
**Selection Bias:**
- *Case Series:* ncfrp_v51 covers only deaths reviewed by fatality review teams and does not capture all SUID cases nationwide.
- *Population Reference:* PRAMS Phase 8 and Phase 7 are based on live births in participating states, missing non-participating areas.

**Information Bias:**
- Variables like weight gain and pre-pregnancy BMI may be measured differently between case series (death records) and population reference surveys.  
- Case series may lack detail due to incomplete or delayed reporting compared to PRAMS surveys.

**Confounding:**
- Unmeasured confounders such as socioeconomic status, access to healthcare, and environmental factors could influence both weight gain/BMI and SUID risk.

#### 6. What the Design Cannot Establish
This design can only establish associations within the case series and cannot estimate incidence rates or causal relationships between maternal weight gain during pregnancy and pre-pregnancy BMI in relation to SUID risk.

#### 7. Prior Art
- **Related Claims:**
  - *suid_literature_abstracts.C10:* Maternal obesity is a risk factor for Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID), with an increased risk associated with higher maternal BMI.
  - This claim addresses the relevance of pre-pregnancy BMI to SUID, but does not examine weight gain during pregnancy.

- **Open Question:**
  The relationship between weight gain during pregnancy and its interaction with pre-pregnancy BMI in affecting SUID risk remains unexplored. While prior work indicates that maternal obesity increases SUID risk, the specific impact of varying levels of weight gain within different pre-pregnancy BMI categories is not addressed.

#### 8. Verdict
**Can these data answer a version of the question?**
- **Yes**, but with limitations:
  - This design can compare proportions or odds of maternal weight gain during pregnancy and pre-pregnancy BMI among SUID cases.
  - It cannot establish causation, nor estimate incidence rates.

**Data Elements Missing:**
- A denominator source to calculate rates or risks is missing in this case-only study.  
- Additional variables capturing socioeconomic status, healthcare access, and environmental factors could help control for confounders but are not available here.
