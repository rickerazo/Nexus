### Design

**Question Restated:** How does maternal pre-pregnancy health care utilization relate to the incidence of Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID) in infants? This study aims to estimate outcome rates by exposure group using a case-specific rate design. The numerator will be SUID cases from a case series, and the denominator will come from live births stratified by level of pre-pregnancy healthcare utilization.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51 (National Fatality Review Case Reporting System)
  - Why: This source is specifically designed to capture child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams, including SUID cases.
  
- **Population Reference:** prams_p7_arf and prams_p8_arf
  - Why: These sources provide survey-based information on mothers' pre-pregnancy health care utilization through weighted samples of live births. The surveys include items about healthcare visits before pregnancy.

- **Denominator Source:** cdc_natl15 (CDC natality public-use file)
  - Why: This source provides the total number of live births in the United States, which is necessary for calculating population-at-risk denominators stratified by pre-pregnancy healthcare utilization levels.

### Period

**Years Needed:**
- The manuscript claims indicate a need to cover trends over multiple years. For consistency and comparability across sources, we aim to use data from 2015 onward.
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** 2014-2019
- **Population Reference (prams_p7_arf & prams_p8_arf):** Phase 7: 2012-2015, Phase 8: 2016-2021 
- **Denominator Source (cdc_natl15):** 2015

### Variables

#### Pre-pregnancy Health Care Utilization
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):**
  - ISSprechronicdiseasebef: Chronic disease control Education - before pregnancy
  
- **Population Reference (prams_p7_arf, prams_p8_arf):**
  - PRE_CKBP: Pre-pregnancy check for high blood pressure
  - PRE_CKD: Pre-pregnancy check for diabetes
  - PRE_RX: Pre-pregnancy prescription medications
  - PRE_EXER: Pre-pregnancy exercise 3+ days/wk

#### Incidence of SUID
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):**
  - ISSprechronicdiseasebef: Chronic disease control Education - before pregnancy (as an indirect measure)
  
- **Denominator Source (cdc_natl15 & prams_p7_arf, prams_p8_arf):**
  - Live births stratified by pre-pregnancy healthcare utilization levels.

### Biases

#### Selection Bias
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** The cases are reviewed by participating fatality review teams and may not be nationally representative.
  
#### Information Bias
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** Reporting on pre-pregnancy health care utilization might differ from self-reported data in PRAMS surveys.
- **Population Reference (prams_p7_arf, prams_p8_arf):** The survey responses may not accurately reflect actual behaviors and practices.

#### Confounding
- **Maternal Characteristics:** Other factors like smoking during pregnancy, socioeconomic status, and other health conditions are likely to be confounders. These need to be controlled for in the analysis.
  
### What the Design Cannot Establish

1. **Causation:** The design can only establish associations due to its observational nature.
2. **Detailed Mechanisms:** It cannot provide detailed mechanisms linking pre-pregnancy healthcare utilization and SUID incidence.

### Prior Art

- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10:** Discusses limitations of vital-record data, which applies here as well.
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C17-C24:** Highlight various limitations related to observational studies and indirect denominators, similar issues apply here.
  
The closest claims focus on maternal smoking and other risk factors but do not directly address pre-pregnancy healthcare utilization.

### Verdict

**Can these sources answer a version of the question?**

Yes. The proposed study design can utilize the available data to estimate associations between maternal pre-pregnancy health care utilization and SUID incidence using an exposure-specific rate approach. However, it is important to acknowledge that this design cannot establish causation or detailed mechanisms.

### Limitations

- **Representativeness:** The case series may not be fully representative of all US cases.
- **Measurement Differences:** Differences in how pre-pregnancy healthcare utilization is measured across sources need careful harmonization.
- **Temporal Alignment:** Ensure that the exposure data from PRAMS surveys aligns temporally with SUID events.

### Next Steps

1. **Data Harmonization:** Standardize measurements of pre-pregnancy health care utilization across PRAMS phases and case series.
2. **Statistical Analysis Plan:** Develop a plan to control for confounding factors such as smoking, socioeconomic status, etc.
3. **Acquisition of Additional Years:** Acquire data from 2015 onwards consistently across all sources if needed.

This study design addresses the research question but requires careful handling of biases and limitations inherent in observational epidemiology.
