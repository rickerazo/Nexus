### Design

**Question:** How do health care visits during pregnancy differ between SUID cases and the general population?

This is a case-population comparison, where we compare the exposure (healthcare visits during pregnancy) among SUID cases from a case series with its prevalence in the general population from a population reference source.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51
  - Covers: Child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams.
  - Used for: Recording SUID cases and their prenatal health care visits (INFprenatal).

- **Population Reference:** prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf
  - Covers: Weighted survey of mothers a few months after live births, sampled from birth certificates in participating states.
  - Used for: Providing weighted prevalence of prenatal healthcare visits among the general population (PNC_VST).

- **Denominator:** cdc_natl15
  - Not used directly but helps understand overall birth patterns and rates.

### Period

**Period:** The manuscript uses data from 2008 to 2019 for ncfrp_v51, and 2016-2021 for prams_p8_arf. Since these periods do not overlap perfectly (ncfrp_v51 goes back to 2008), the analysis should focus on the overlapping period of 2016-2019.

### Variables

**Healthcare Visits during Pregnancy:**

- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):**
  - **INFprenatal:** Number of prenatal visits.
  
- **Population Reference (prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf):**
  - **PNC_VST:** Weighted number of prenatal care visits.

**Measurement Differences:**

- **ncfrp_v51** measures the actual number of prenatal visits for mothers who had SUID cases.
- **prams_p8_arf and prams_p7_arf** measure the weighted prevalence of prenatal visits among a representative sample of live births.

### Biases

#### Selection Bias:
- The case series (ncfrp_v51) only captures deaths reviewed by fatality review teams, which may not be fully representative of all SUID cases.
  
#### Information Bias:
- **Case Series:** Direct report from the mothers after a child death might influence recall or reporting accuracy compared to the population reference source.
- **Population Reference:** Survey data can have non-response bias and underreporting due to social desirability or lack of knowledge.

#### Confounding:
- Factors such as socioeconomic status, education level, maternal age, and other health conditions could confound the relationship between prenatal visits and SUID risk. These factors need adjustment in analysis.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

The design can only compare the prevalence of prenatal healthcare visits between SUID cases and a representative sample of live births but cannot establish causality or estimate incidence rates directly. Additionally, it does not account for postnatal exposures that may influence outcomes (e.g., breastfeeding practices).

### Prior Art

- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10 [limitation]:** The analysis excludes a small number of deaths at the extremes of the PCA distribution.
  
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C23 [hypothesis]:** Chronic intermittent hypoxia alters local respiratory circuit function at the level of the pre-Bötzinger complex.

The closest claim is that previous studies have examined various risk factors for SUID but do not directly address healthcare utilization during pregnancy. The manuscript's findings on developmental timing and risk factors (e.g., bed sharing, maternal smoking) suggest that prenatal care might play a role, but it has not been explicitly tested in prior work.

### Verdict

**These data can partially answer the research question but with limitations:**

1. **Common Period:** The analysis period should be limited to 2016-2019.
2. **Measurement Differences:** Direct comparisons between case series and population reference need careful adjustment for biases.
3. **Biases and Confounding:** Adjusting for confounders is necessary.
4. **Causality:** Cannot establish causation, only associations.

The data can be used to compare prenatal healthcare utilization in SUID cases versus the general population but should be interpreted with caution due to potential biases and limited causal inference.
