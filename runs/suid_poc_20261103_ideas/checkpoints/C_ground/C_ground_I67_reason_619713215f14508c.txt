### Design

**Question:** How does hospital overnight stay frequency differ between mothers of SUID cases and live births?

**Design:** Measurement comparison. Compare how the same concept is recorded in two or more sources (wording, timing, who reports it, how missing answers are coded) to assess differences that would bias a comparison between them.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:**
  - ncfrp_v51
  - Reason: This dataset records child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams and includes SUID cases. It does not include live births as denominators.
  
- **Population Reference:**
  - prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf
  - Reason: These datasets survey mothers after live births (no infant deaths included) to provide population reference data.

### Period

**Period:** 
- ncfrp_v51 covers child deaths from 2004-2019. However, the manuscript focuses on SUID cases reviewed by fatality review teams in a period that is not clearly specified beyond 2008-2019.
- prams_p8_arf covers Phase 8 (2016-2021).
- prams_p7_arf covers Phase 7 (2012-2015).

The common period for all sources would be from 2012 to 2019, covering the latest available data in both population references and case series.

### Variables

**Concept:** Hospital Overnight Stay Frequency

- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):**
  - Variable: INF4hosprtnnum
    - Number of nights infant stayed overnight at hospital.
    - Missing answers are documented per item (9 = unknown or not stated).

- **Population Reference (prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf):**
  - Variable: LTH_HOSP
    - Length of time baby stayed in the hospital.
    - Values:
      - 1 = < 1 day
      - 2 = 1-2 days
      - 3 = 3-5 days
      - Missing answers are documented per item (.B = don't know or blank, .S = skipped).

**Notes:**
- ncfrp_v51 provides a count of nights while prams_p8_arf and prams_p7_arf provide categories for the length of stay.
- The timing of recording differs (ncfrp_v51 is at death, whereas PRAMS surveys are conducted a few months after birth).
- Missing data handling varies between sources.

### Biases

**Selection Bias:**
- ncfrp_v51 includes only reviewed SUID cases and not live births as denominators.
- PRAMS samples mothers of live births from participating states, which may differ in geographic distribution and demographic characteristics compared to the case series.

**Information Bias:**
- ncfrp_v51 captures hospital stays related specifically to infant deaths, whereas PRAMS captures general postpartum hospital stays for live births.
- The timing of data collection (at death vs. a few months after birth) introduces differences in the measurement of postnatal hospital stays.

**Confounding:**
- Differences in geographic distribution, socioeconomic status, and healthcare access between states participating in PRAMS may introduce confounding when comparing to SUID cases reviewed by fatality review teams.
  
### What the Design Cannot Establish

This design can only establish a comparison of how hospital overnight stay frequency is recorded differently between case series (SUID) and population reference data (live births). It cannot estimate rates, risks, or incidence differences due to the absence of a denominator source for SUID cases.

### Prior Art

- **Claim C13 from bedsharing_ms_v7:** This claim discusses future research directions related to understanding postnatal exposures like separation from the mother. However, it does not directly address hospital overnight stays.
  
- No claims in manuscript or literature abstracts are closely aligned with the proposed study question.

### Verdict

**Verdict:**
The data can be used to compare how hospital overnight stay frequency is recorded differently between SUID cases and live births but cannot establish incidence differences due to missing denominator information for SUID cases. The design requires additional sources that provide a denominator of live births in relation to SUID cases.

### Missing Data Element
- **Denominator Source:** A source providing both numerator (SUID cases) and denominator (live births) is required to estimate rates or risk differences.
