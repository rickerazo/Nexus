### Design

**Rephrased Question:**
Does hospital stay length after birth correlate with the risk of Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID) in a population sample? Specifically, do infants who had longer or shorter hospital stays have different rates of SUID compared to those with average hospital stays?

**Design Type:** Exposure-specific rates. Estimate outcome rates by exposure group using cases from a case series (numerator), the exposure prevalence from a population reference source, and the population at risk from a denominator source.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series Source:**
  - **ncfrp_v51**: Provides SUID cases.
  - **Rationale**: This dataset contains detailed information on child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams, making it suitable for identifying SUID cases.
  
- **Population Reference Source:**
  - **prams_p7_arf** and **prams_p8_arf**: Provide data from surveys of mothers a few months after live births.
  - **Rationale**: PRAMS collects detailed information on maternal health, behaviors, and infant care practices that can serve as exposure measures.

- **Denominator Source:**
  - **cdc_linkco15** or **cdc_natl15**: Provides the denominator for SUID incidence (i.e., live births in a given year).
  - **Rationale**: CDC natality files provide comprehensive data on live births, which is necessary to calculate rates of SUID.

### Period

- **Period:** The period that lies within the coverage of all sources is 2016-2019. This allows for sufficient overlap between PRAMS Phase 8 (2016-2021), NCFRP data (2014-2019), and CDC natality files (2015).

### Variables

**Exposure: Hospital Stay Length**

- **Case Series Source (ncfrp_v51):**
  - Variable: `INF4infhospdays`: Total number of days infant hospitalized
  - Missing answers: Unknown is documented (e.g., `UNK`), and blanks are treated as unknowns.

**Outcome: SUID Incidence**

- **Case Series Source (ncfrp_v51):**
  - Variable: Any entry in the dataset that identifies a case as an SUID.
  - Missing answers: Cases missing details on cause of death or hospital stay length are excluded.

**Exposure Prevalence from Population Reference Sources**

- **PRAMS Phase 8 (prams_p8_arf) and PRAMS Phase 7 (prams_p7_arf):**
  - Variable: `LTH_HOSP`: Length of time baby spent in the hospital.
  - Missing answers: .B = Don't know or blank, .S = Skipped; these are not counted as exposed.

**Population at Risk from Denominator Source**

- **CDC Natality Files (cdc_linkco15/cdc_natl15):**
  - Live births in the period of interest.
  - Missing answers: Documented per item and treated accordingly.

### Biases

**Selection Bias:**

- The NCFRP cases may not represent all SUID deaths due to varying participation rates across states.
- PRAMS samples are biased towards higher-risk populations, potentially affecting exposure measurements.

**Information Bias:**

- Hospital stay length may be reported inaccurately in both case series and population reference sources.
- Maternal recall bias can affect the accuracy of hospital stay lengths reported in PRAMS surveys.

**Confounding:**

- Confounders like prematurity, maternal smoking, socioeconomic status, and other risk factors for SUID need to be controlled for in analyses.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

The design cannot establish causation; it only estimates rates and associations. The correlation between hospital stay length and SUID incidence does not imply that longer stays cause higher risks of SUID.

### Prior Art

- **Claim C13 from `bedsharing_ms_v7`:** Postconceptional age (PCA) may clarify how bed sharing, maternal smoking, and prematurity relate to Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID).
  - This claim explores the timing of risk factors but does not directly address hospital stay length.
- **Claim C10 from `bedsharing_ms_v7`:** The study uses vital-record data, which may have exposure and cause-of-death misclassification issues.

### Verdict

These data can answer a version of the question: Does hospital stay length after birth correlate with SUID incidence? However, several limitations exist:

- **Selection Bias:** NCFRP captures only reviewed cases, leading to potential underreporting.
- **Information Bias:** Maternal recall and reporting errors in PRAMS surveys need careful handling.
- **Confounding Factors:** Prematurity, maternal smoking, socioeconomic status, and other risk factors must be controlled for.

To address these limitations:
1. Include comprehensive covariates from both case series and population references to control confounders.
2. Use multiple imputation techniques for missing data where appropriate.
3. Perform sensitivity analyses to assess the robustness of findings under varying assumptions about selection bias and information accuracy.

Overall, the data can be used to estimate rates but should be interpreted with caution regarding causation due to potential biases and limitations in data collection methods.
