### Study Design for Investigating Trends in Hospital Overnight Stays for SUID Cases Compared to the Population

#### 1. The Design
**Restated Question:** 
Is there a trend over time in the proportion of Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID) cases involving hospital overnight stays compared to the general population of live births?

**Design Type:**
Trends within cases. Compare proportions among the cases across periods. When a case series' coverage changes over time, changes in case counts are not changes in incidence.

#### 2. Sources by Role
- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51 (NFR-CRS)
  - Why: Contains detailed information on SUID cases and hospital stays.
  
- **Population Reference:** prams_p7_arf 
  - Why: Provides data from a population-based survey of mothers, including variables related to health care utilization after delivery.

#### 3. Period
**Period:** 2012–2015

**Justification for the period choice:**
- The PRAMS Phase 7 (prams_p7_arf) covers this time range.
- ncfrp_v51 data are available from 2008 onwards, but we need to align with PRAMS' coverage.

#### 4. Variables
| Concept                  | Candidate Variable(s)                          | Wording/Timing/Who Reports It/Coding of Missing Answers |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Hospital Overnight Stay  | ncfrp_v51:INF4hosprtn (Yes, infant returned to hospital overnight after delivery) <br> ncfrp_v51:INF4infhospdays (Total number of days infant hospitalized) | Reported by the reviewing fatality review team; missing coded as unknown. |
| Health Care Utilization  | prams_p7_arf:LTH_HOSP (Length of hospital stay for baby) <br> prams_p7_arf:PNC_VST (Number of prenatal care visits) | Surveyed mothers a few months post-delivery; missing coded as unknown or not stated. |
| Month Prenatal Care Began| ncfrp_v51:PRECARE5 (Month prenatal care began recode); prams_p7_arf:PNC_MTH (Month of first prenatal care visit) | Reported by medical records and survey, respectively; missing coded as unknown or no prenatal care. |

#### 5. Biases
**Selection Bias:** 
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** The NFR-CRS is subject to varying participation rates across states and over time. Changes in case counts may reflect changes in reporting rather than incidence.
  
**Information Bias:**
- **Hospital Overnight Stay:** Reporting by fatality review teams, which might differ from actual occurrences due to incomplete or inconsistent documentation.
- **Health Care Utilization (PNC visits):** Reported by mothers a few months post-delivery and could be subject to recall bias.

**Confounding:**
- **Potential Confounders Not Accounted for:** Health insurance status, socioeconomic factors, maternal health conditions such as smoking and obesity. These variables are not available in the case series or population reference data used here.

#### 6. What the Design Cannot Establish
The design can only compare trends within SUID cases and cannot establish incidence rates or causal relationships between hospital overnight stays and SUID risk. The lack of a denominator source precludes estimating overall risk in the general population.

#### 7. Prior Art: 
- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10:** Exclusion of extremely premature infants may introduce bias but does not address trends in postnatal care.
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C8, C11:** Focuses on the association between bed sharing and SUID incidence across PCA windows but does not address trends in hospital overnight stays.

#### 8. Verdict
**Can these data answer a version of the question?**

Yes, but with limitations:
- The design can assess trends in hospital overnight stays among SUID cases compared to health care utilization patterns reported by mothers from a population reference.
- However, due to missing denominators and potential biases (selection, information), definitive causal claims or incidence estimates cannot be made.

**What is Missing:**
- **Denominator Source:** A denominator source to estimate overall hospital overnight stays in the general live birth population would strengthen the analysis.
- **Confounding Variables:** Data on maternal health insurance status, socioeconomic factors, and other potential confounders are needed for a more comprehensive risk assessment.
