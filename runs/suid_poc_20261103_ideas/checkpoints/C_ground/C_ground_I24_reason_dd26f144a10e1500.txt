### Design

**Question:** What is the incidence of Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID) among infants born via cesarean delivery compared to vaginal delivery?

**Design Type:** Exposure-specific rates. Estimate outcome rates by exposure group using cases from a case series, exposure prevalence from a population reference source, and population at risk from a denominator source.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:**
  - ncfrp_v51 (NFR-CRS): This source provides the SUID cases.
  
- **Population Reference:**
  - prams_p7_arf, prams_p8_arf: These sources provide exposure prevalence from a survey of mothers of live births.

- **Denominator:**
  - cdc_linkco15, cdc_natl15: These sources provide the population at risk (all US live births).

### Period

**Common Period:** The manuscript claims and data cover various years. To ensure comparability, we need a common period that lies within all source coverage:
- ncfrp_v51: 2008-2019
- prams_p7_arf: 2012-2015
- cdc_linkco15, cdc_natl15: 2015

**Common Period Chosen:** 2015 (the most recent year covered by all sources).

### Variables

#### Outcome (SUID)
- **Case Series Source:**
  - ncfrp_v51: N/A (cases only, no denominator information)

#### Exposure (Delivery Method)
- **Population Reference Sources:**
  - prams_p7_arf: DEL_VAG (Vaginal Delivery? [U=UNKNOWN; 1=NO (UNCHECKED); 2=YES (CHECKED)])
  - prams_p8_arf: DEL_VAG (Vaginal Delivery [.N=NOT RECORDED; .U=UNKNOWN; 1=NO (UNCHECKED); 2=YES (CHECKED)])

- **Denominator Sources:**
  - cdc_linkco15, cdc_natl15:
    - RDMETH_REC (Delivery Method Recode [1=Vaginal (excludes vaginal after previous C-section); 2=Vaginal after previous c-section; 3=Primary C-section; 4=Repeat C-section …])
    - DMETH_REC (Delivery Method Recode Combined [1=Vaginal; 2=C-Section; 9=Unknown])

### Biases

#### Selection Bias
- **Case Series:** The ncfrp_v51 captures only reviewed SUID cases, which may not be representative of all SUIDs. Participation in fatality review teams varies by state.
- **Population Reference:** PRAMS samples mothers from live births, excluding those who experienced stillbirth or infant death.

#### Information Bias
- **Case Series:** Ascertainment and reporting practices vary among jurisdictions, potentially affecting consistency.
- **Denominator Source:** The CDC files use vital records that may have misclassification issues for exposure variables (e.g., smoking status).

#### Confounding
- Factors like maternal age, education level, socioeconomic status, and preterm birth are likely confounders. These factors differ between vaginal and cesarean deliveries.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

The design can estimate relative risk but not establish causation due to its observational nature. It cannot account for time-varying exposures after delivery or other unmeasured confounders.

### Prior Art

**Manuscript Claims:**
- **SUID Manuscripts:** No claim directly compares cesarean and vaginal deliveries.
- **Bedsharing Manuscripts (bedsharing_ms_v7):** Examines associations with bed sharing, maternal smoking, and prematurity across developmental windows but does not address mode of delivery.

The question remains open as no previous work has specifically compared SUID incidence between cesarean and vaginal deliveries using the proposed data sources.

### Verdict

These data can be used to estimate the relative risk of SUID among infants born via cesarean versus vaginal delivery in 2015. However, they cannot establish causation or address time-varying exposures post-delivery. The design is feasible with the given data but requires careful consideration of biases and confounding factors.

### Additional Considerations

- **Missing Data:** Handle missing values appropriately (e.g., unknowns and skips).
- **Survey Weights:** Use PRAMS survey weights for accurate population-level estimates.
- **Comparability:** Ensure comparability in how delivery methods are coded across sources.
