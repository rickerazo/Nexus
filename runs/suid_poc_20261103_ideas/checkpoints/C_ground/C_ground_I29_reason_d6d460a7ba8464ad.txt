### Design

**Question:** Is there an increased risk of SUID in infants whose mothers did not receive adequate prenatal care compared to those who received adequate care?

**Design:** Case-case comparison. Compare cases of SUID from the ncfrp_v51 case series with other infant deaths within the same source (ncfrp_v51) to assess whether prenatal care adequacy is associated with SUID.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:**
  - Source: `ncfrp_v51` (National Fatality Review Case Reporting System)
  - Why: This dataset contains cases of child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams and includes information on various factors contributing to these deaths, including prenatal care adequacy.

- **Population Reference:**
  - Source: `prams_p8_arf` (PRAMS Phase 8 Analytic Research File)
  - Why: This dataset provides a weighted survey of mothers who had live births a few months after the birth and can be used to assess prenatal care adequacy among mothers of living infants.

- **Denominator:**
  - Source: `cdc_linkco15` (CDC linked birth/infant death public-use file)
  - Why: This dataset provides both SUID cases and denominators for live births in the same year, allowing for a comparison of proportions or odds between SUID cases and non-SUID cases.

### Period

**Period:** The common period covered by all sources is 2015 to 2019. However, since `prams_p8_arf` covers years from 2016-2021, the comparison will be limited to the overlapping period of 2016-2019.

### Variables

**Prenatal Care Adequacy:**
- **Case Series (`ncfrp_v51`):**
  - `INF4visnum`: Number of primary care provider visits.
  - `INFisscare`: Access or compliance issues related to prenatal care.
  
- **Population Reference (`prams_p8_arf`):**
  - `PNC_VST`: Number of prenatal care visit (with unknown values coded as `.U=UNKNOWN`).
  - `PREV_PRE`: Previous preterm births (FORCED SKIP; `.B=DK/BLANK`, `.S=SKIP`).

### Biases

**Selection Bias:**
- **Case Series (`ncfrp_v51`):** Only includes child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams, which may not be a representative sample of all infant deaths.
- **Population Reference (`prams_p8_arf`):** Survey data from PRAMS is weighted and designed to represent mothers with live births in participating states but does not include information on stillbirths or neonatal deaths.

**Information Bias:**
- **Case Series (`ncfrp_v51`):** Reporting of prenatal care details may be incomplete or inaccurate due to the nature of case reviews.
- **Population Reference (`prams_p8_arf`):** Survey data may suffer from recall bias and missing values (`.B=DK/BLANK`, `.S=SKIP`).

**Confounding:**
- Other factors such as maternal smoking, socioeconomic status, and infant health conditions not adequately controlled for in the analysis.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

The case-case comparison design can only establish associations between prenatal care adequacy and SUID cases versus other deaths within ncfrp_v51. It cannot estimate incidence rates or risk ratios due to the lack of a denominator source that includes both live births and infant deaths for the same population.

### Prior Art

**Manuscript Claims:**
- **SUID Manuscripts:** C8, C9, C10 discuss prenatal care factors but do not specifically address adequacy.
- **Bedsharing Manuscripts:** Focus on postconceptional age (PCA) and associations with SUID incidence, maternal smoking, prematurity, and bed sharing.

None of the claims directly test whether inadequate prenatal care is associated with increased risk of SUID compared to adequate prenatal care. This makes the proposed study a novel contribution that builds upon existing literature by focusing specifically on prenatal care adequacy as a factor in SUID incidence.

### Verdict

**Can these data answer the version of the question?**

Yes, but with limitations:
- The design can establish an association between prenatal care adequacy and SUID cases compared to other infant deaths within `ncfrp_v51`.
- However, due to the lack of a denominator source that includes both live births and neonatal deaths for the same population during 2016-2019, it cannot estimate incidence rates or risk ratios.
- The analysis would need to be careful about selection bias from the case series and information bias in survey data.

**Data Element Missing:**
A denominator source that includes both live births and neonatal deaths for the same population during 2016-2019 is needed to estimate incidence rates or risk ratios.
