### Design

**Design:**
Case-case comparison. Compare the cases of SUID (Sudden Unexpected Infant Death) with other causes of infant death within the same case series, to identify factors specific to SUID.

**Restated Question:**
What are the differences in pregnancy weight gain between infants who died from SUID and those who died from other causes?

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51
  - **Why:** This source includes both SUID cases and other infant deaths reviewed by fatality review teams.
  
- **Population Reference:** prams_p8_arf (for survey weight) & prams_p8_std2
  - **Why:** The population reference provides data on pregnancy-related factors from a representative sample of mothers, necessary for comparing proportions with survey weights.

### Period

**Period:**
The common period within the coverage of both ncfrp_v51 and prams_p8_arf/prams_p8_std2 is 2016-2019. This ensures that data from both sources are contemporaneous, allowing for valid comparisons.

### Variables

1. **Pregnancy Weight Gain:**
   - **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** INF4momwtgnunk & INF4momwtgn
     - Reporting Flag and actual weight gain/loss in pounds.
   - **Population Reference (prams_p8_std2):** PGWTGN8, PGWTGNU8_DK, PGWTGNU8
     - Weight gain during pregnancy, units of measurement, and unknown responses.

2. **Pre-pregnancy Weight:**
   - **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** INF4mompregwtunk & INF4mompregwt
     - Pre-pregnancy weight in pounds.
   - **Population Reference (prams_p8_arf/prams_p7_arf):** MOM_LBKG, MAT_PRWT
     - Surveyed pre-pregnancy weight.

3. **Other Pregnancy-related Factors:**
   - **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):** INF4pregnotalive, INF3previnfant, INF3lbwt
     - Previous fetal loss/miscarriage, infant > 4000 grams, low birth weight.
   - **Population Reference (prams_p8_arf/prams_p7_arf):** OTH_TERM, PRE_LB_NAPHSIS, PREGHX
     - Number of other pregnancy outcomes, previous live births grouped by weight intervals.

### Biases

1. **Selection Bias:**
   - The case series from ncfrp_v51 includes only deaths reviewed by fatality review teams, which may not be representative of all infant deaths.
   - The population reference (PRAMS) covers a weighted sample of mothers in participating states, potentially missing some geographic areas or subpopulations.

2. **Information Bias:**
   - Pregnancy weight gain and pre-pregnancy weight are reported differently between case series and population references:
     - Case series may have incomplete data due to the nature of fatality review reports.
     - PRAMS surveys can suffer from recall bias, as mothers are surveyed months after birth.

3. **Confounding:**
   - Other factors such as maternal age, smoking history, socioeconomic status (SES), and health conditions could confound the relationship between pregnancy weight gain and SUID/other causes of infant death.
   - These variables need to be controlled for in analyses.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

The design cannot establish causation or estimate incidence rates due to its case-case nature. It can only compare proportions or odds ratios between groups, identifying factors that are more prevalent among SUID cases compared to other causes of infant death.

### Prior Art

- **Manuscript Claims:**
  - No claims in the manuscripts directly address pregnancy weight gain as a risk factor for SUID.
  - The closest claim is C10 (SUID_manuscript_v9_clean) which mentions maternal smoking, but not pregnancy weight gain. 
  - Other studies focus on postconceptional age and bed sharing (bedsharing_ms_v7), further emphasizing the need to investigate other potentially important factors like weight gain.

- **Literature Abstracts:**
  - No abstracts in suid_literature_abstracts.C1-C62 mention pregnancy weight gain as a risk factor for SUID.
  - Some studies discuss maternal obesity and smoking, but not explicitly pregnancy weight gain.

### Verdict

**Verdict:**

These data can answer the revised question by comparing pregnancy weight gain between SUID cases and other infant deaths using ncfrp_v51 as the case series source and prams_p8_arf/prams_p8_std2 for population reference comparisons. However, due to the limitations of selection bias, information bias, and confounding factors, the results should be interpreted with caution regarding causation or incidence estimation.

**Key Missing Elements:**
- Direct linkage between PRAMS and case series data would strengthen comparability.
- Additional control variables (e.g., maternal smoking, SES) are needed for robust adjustment in analyses.
