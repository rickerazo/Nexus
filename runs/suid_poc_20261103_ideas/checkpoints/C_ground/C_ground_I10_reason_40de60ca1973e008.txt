### Design

**Question Restated:** What is the difference in smoking cessation before pregnancy between SUID cases and a population reference?

**Design Type:** Case-population comparison.

**Case Series Source (SUID Cases):**
- **Source:** ncfrp_v51 (National Fatality Review Case Reporting System)
- **Role:** To provide information on SUID cases, specifically maternal smoking cessation before pregnancy.
- **Selection Bias:** Selection bias is present because the case series only includes child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams and does not account for all infant deaths in the population.

**Population Reference Source (PRAMS Mothers):**
- **Source:** prams_p8_arf (Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System - Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System)
- **Role:** To provide a weighted sample of live births to estimate smoking cessation before pregnancy among women who have had a recent live birth.
- **Selection Bias:** PRAMS surveys are designed for live births and not deaths, potentially missing high-risk groups like those who experience infant mortality.

**Denominator Source (Births):**
- **Source:** None required as this is a comparison of proportions between two groups rather than incidence rates.

### Period

The study period needs to cover the years 2008-2019 for both SUID cases and PRAMS data. The ncfrp_v51 covers these years, while prams_p8_arf also includes data from this time frame. No additional years need to be acquired as all necessary data is available.

### Variables

**Concept:** Smoking cessation before pregnancy
- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):**
  - Variable: `INFsmbefpregcig` (If yes, smoke before pregnancy, number cigarettes)
  - Missing Answers: Documented per item; unknown quantity coded as "unknown" or exposed.
  
- **Population Reference (prams_p8_arf):**
  - Variable: `SMK63B_A` (VAR: YES/NO SMOKE 3 BEF PREG)
  - Missing Answers: Blank or don't know (`.B=DK/BLANK`)
  - Weighted Prevalence: Use WTANAL survey weight for population prevalence.

**Differences in Measurement:**
- **Case Series:** Reports on smoking habits before pregnancy, focusing specifically on the number of cigarettes smoked.
- **Population Reference:** Binary measure indicating whether the mother smoked in the three months prior to pregnancy (yes/no).

### Biases

1. **Selection Bias:**
   - **Case Series:** Only includes deaths reviewed by fatality review teams and may not represent all infant deaths.
   - **Population Reference:** PRAMS is a survey of live births, potentially excluding high-risk groups like those who experience infant mortality.

2. **Information Bias:**
   - **Case Series:** Detailed smoking cessation history before pregnancy but lacks information on post-pregnancy behavior changes due to case review limitations.
   - **Population Reference:** May include recall bias as mothers are surveyed months after birth regarding their pre-pregnancy smoking habits.

3. **Confounding Factors:**
   - **Case Series & Population Reference:** Potential confounders like socioeconomic status, education level, and access to healthcare may differ between groups and impact smoking cessation rates.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

- The design cannot establish causation or temporal relationships between smoking cessation before pregnancy and SUID risk. It only compares proportions between cases and a population reference.
- No denominator source is used, so incidence rates or risks cannot be calculated; this limits understanding of the absolute impact on infant mortality.

### Prior Art

The manuscript claims indicate that maternal smoking during pregnancy has been established as a significant risk factor for SUID (claims C9, C10 from bedsharing_ms_v7). However, there is no specific mention of smoking cessation before pregnancy. Therefore, this study would provide new insights into the effectiveness of pre-pregnancy smoking cessation programs.

### Verdict

**Can these sources answer the question?**

Yes, these data can support a comparison between SUID cases and PRAMS mothers to assess differences in smoking cessation behaviors before pregnancy. However, several limitations must be acknowledged:
- **Selection Bias:** The case series may not fully represent all infant deaths.
- **Information Bias:** Recall bias exists for PRAMS data on pre-pregnancy habits.
- **Confounding Factors:** Socioeconomic and healthcare access disparities could affect results.

Despite these challenges, the design is feasible with the available data sources.
