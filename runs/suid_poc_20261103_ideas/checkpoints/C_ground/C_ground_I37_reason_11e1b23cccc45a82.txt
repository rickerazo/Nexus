### Design

**Question Restated:** Is the prevalence of prenatal complications higher among infants who experienced SUID compared to those with other fetal or neonatal conditions, as recorded in the NFR-CRS (ncfrp_v51)?

**Design:** Case-case comparison. Compare cases of SUID from ncfrp_v51 against cases categorized as congenital anomalies, birth injuries, respiratory distress syndrome, or prematurity within the same case series.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51 (2014-2019)
  - Cases of SUID and other fetal/neonatal conditions are recorded here.
  - No denominator source; this design can only compare proportions or odds among cases, not incidence or risk.

### Period

**Years Needed:**
- ncfrp_v51 covers 2014-2019. More years of case data could be acquired if necessary to increase statistical power but are currently available and sufficient for the analysis period.

### Variables

#### Prenatal Complications
- **ncfrp_v51:** 
  - INF3medical: During pregnancy did mother have medical complications/infections (Yes/No)
  - INF4pretermlabref: Medical complications - Preterm labor - Referral
  - INF4moveref: Medical complications - Decreased fetal movement - Referral

#### Other Conditions
- **ncfrp_v51:**
  - ISSinfconganom: Congenital anomaly (Yes/No)
  - ISSinfbirthinjury: Birth injury (Yes/No)
  - ISSinfprematurity: Prematurity (Yes/No)

### Biases

#### Selection Bias
- **ncfrp_v51:** Participation by reviewing states may introduce selection bias, but the manuscript uses a subset of years with stable participation levels (2008-2019).

#### Information Bias
- Different case series record prenatal complications and other conditions differently. ncfrp_v51 captures these through various detailed medical history items, while PRAMS surveys focus on self-reported maternal health during pregnancy.

#### Confounding
- No denominator source means the comparison does not account for population-level confounders such as socioeconomic status or environmental exposures that could influence prenatal complications and outcomes.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

- The design cannot establish incidence or risk of SUID because it lacks a denominator. It can only compare proportions of cases with prenatal complications.
- No causal inference can be made due to observational nature.

### Prior Art

None of the manuscript claims directly address the specific comparison between SUID and other fetal/neonatal conditions regarding prenatal complications. The closest claim is:
- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10 [limitation]:** The analysis excludes a small number of deaths at the extremes of the PCA distribution, including some extremely and very preterm infants who died early in postnatal life.
  - This limitation highlights that while case data includes various conditions, it does not directly compare SUID to other conditions regarding prenatal complications.

### Verdict

These sources can answer whether the prevalence of prenatal complications differs between SUID cases and those with congenital anomalies, birth injuries, respiratory distress syndrome, or prematurity within ncfrp_v51. The design is feasible but limited in scope due to the absence of a denominator source for calculating incidence or risk.

### Conclusion

Yes, these sources can be used to conduct a case-case comparison to determine if prenatal complications are more prevalent among infants who experienced SUID compared to those with other fetal/neonatal conditions as recorded in ncfrp_v51. However, due to the absence of a denominator source, this design only allows for comparing proportions and cannot establish incidence or risk directly.
