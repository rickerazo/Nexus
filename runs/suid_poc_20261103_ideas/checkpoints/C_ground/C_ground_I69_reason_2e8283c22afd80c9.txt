### Design

**Restated Question:** Do maternal health problems differ between mothers of SUID cases and those who had live births?

**Design Type:** Measurement comparison. This design will compare how the same concept is recorded in two or more sources to identify potential biases when comparing proportions of maternal health problems between mothers of SUID cases and live birth controls.

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:**
  - **Source:** ncfrp_v51 (NFR-CRS)
  - **Role:** Provides case-level data for infants who died from Sudden Unexpected Infant Death (SUID).

- **Population Reference:**
  - **Sources:** prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf
  - **Role:** Offers population-level survey data of mothers of live births to estimate the prevalence of maternal health problems among a representative sample.

### Period

**Period Range:** The overlap between all sources is from 2016-2019. This period covers both case series (ncfrp_v51) and reference surveys (prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf).

### Variables

**Concept: Maternal Health Conditions**

- **Case Series (ncfrp_v51):**
  - Variable: `INF3prevmedical`: Did mother have medical complications in previous pregnancies
  - Variable: `INF4inftopmsign`: If yes, infant safety topic - Maternal signs warrant medical attn
  - Variable: `INFmothinj`: Mother injured during pregnancy
  - Variable: `INFexpipv`: During pregnancy did mother experience intimate partner violence

- **Population Reference (prams_p8_arf):**
  - Variable: `HTH_HRT`: Health prob -- Heart
  - Variable: `HTH_ASMA`: Health prob -- Asthma
  - Variable: `HTH_ALRGI`: Health prob -- Allergies
  - Variable: `HTH_SEIZ`: Health prob -- Epilepsy

- **Population Reference (prams_p7_arf):**
  - Variable: `MM_NOMD`: BC: No Medical Risk Factors? 

### Biases

**Selection Bias:** 
- The case series includes only those infants who died from SUID, while the population reference is a survey of mothers with live births. This difference in sampling leads to selection bias.
  
**Information Bias:** 
- Differences in how health conditions are reported between sources: Case series (ncfrp_v51) captures medical complications during pregnancy and previous pregnancies through various items, whereas PRAMS surveys focus on specific health problems like heart disease, asthma, allergies, etc.

**Confounding:**
- Confounders such as socioeconomic status, geographic location, and urban/rural settings are not well-matched between the case series (no state names or rural/urban indicators) and population reference (participating sites only).

### What the Design Cannot Establish

This design cannot establish a causal relationship between maternal health problems and SUID. It can only compare proportions of reported health conditions in both groups, which may be biased due to differences in measurement and reporting.

### Prior Art

**Relevant Claims:**

- **suid_literature_abstracts.C10:** Maternal obesity is associated with increased risk for SUID.
- **suid_literature_abstracts.C11:** Maternal smoking during pregnancy significantly increases the risk of SUID.
  
These claims touch on maternal health risks but do not specifically address other medical conditions and their prevalence in mothers of SUID cases versus live births. The proposed study aims to fill this gap by comparing specific health problems.

### Verdict

**Verdict: Yes, these data can answer a version of the question.**

The design uses ncfrp_v51 for case-level SUID cases and prams_p8_arf/prams_p7_arf for population reference surveys among mothers of live births to compare maternal health conditions. However, it is crucial to acknowledge that differences in measurement timing (during pregnancy vs. post-pregnancy), wording, and reporting practices between these sources can introduce biases. This design establishes a comparison but not causation or incidence rates due to the lack of denominator data for SUID cases.

**Missing Element:** 
- A denominator source that provides population-level live birth counts for each state/region would be necessary to calculate true risk/incidence ratios and adjust for potential confounders related to geographic location.
