### Design

**Design:** Trends within cases. Compare proportions among the cases across periods.

**Restated Question:** How has the proportion of SUID cases with hospital overnight stays changed over different time periods (2008-2019 vs. PRAMS data)?

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:**
  - **ncfrp_v51**: This dataset records child deaths reviewed by fatality review teams, including SUID cases. It provides the necessary case details to track hospital overnight stays among SUID cases.

- **Population Reference:**
  - **prams_p8_arf** and **prams_p7_arf**: These datasets are survey-based population references that provide demographic information about live births, including hospital stay lengths and demographic data from mothers. They do not have a denominator role since they do not cover infant deaths.

- **Denominator:**
  - Not applicable for this study as we are comparing proportions among cases rather than estimating incidence rates.

### Period

**Years Needed:** 
- Case Series (ncfrp_v51): 2008-2019
- Population Reference (prams_p8_arf, prams_p7_arf): All years held for these datasets (Phase 8: 2016-2021; Phase 7: 2012-2015)

### Variables

**Concept:** Hospital Overnight Stay
- **ncfrp_v51**: 
  - Variable: `INF4hosprtn`
  - Label: Infant return to hospital overnight after delivery (Yes/No)
  - Value: 1 = Yes, 9 = Unknown or Not Stated
  
**Concept:** Demographic Information
- **prams_p8_arf and prams_p7_arf**: 
  - Variable: `LTH_HOSP`
  - Label: Length of time hospital stay (number of days)
  - Values:
    - 1 = < 1 DAY
    - 2 = 1-2 DAYS
    - 3 = 3-5 DAYS
    - 4 = 6-9 DAYS
    - 5 = 10+ DAYS

**Concept:** Survey Weight for Prevalence Estimation (prams_p8_arf and prams_p7_arf)
- **Variable**: `WTANAL`
  - Label: BC WT: ANALYSIS WT
  - Required to estimate prevalence from PRAMS data.

### Biases

1. **Selection Bias**
   - **Case Series (ncfrp_v51)**: Participation in fatality review teams varies over time, which could affect case counts and trends.
   - **Population Reference (PRAMS)**: Sampling bias as it is a survey of mothers post-live birth and may not represent all live births.

2. **Information Bias**
   - Reporting differences between case series and population reference sources can lead to discrepancies in hospital stay measures. 
   - For example, `INF4hosprtn` in ncfrp_v51 vs. `LTH_HOSP` in PRAMS does not directly align as one is a binary measure while the other measures length.

3. **Confounding**
   - Differences in maternal and infant characteristics over time could confound trends observed in hospital stays.
   - Maternal factors such as socioeconomic status, health conditions, and access to healthcare might influence hospital stay lengths.

### What the Design Cannot Establish

- The design can only compare proportions among SUID cases with hospital overnight stays across different periods. It cannot establish causality or incidence rates due to the lack of a denominator source.
- Differences in case ascertainment over time may affect observed trends but are not directly accounted for by this comparison.

### Prior Art

The closest related claims do not address hospital overnight stay trends among SUID cases:
- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C6**: Focuses on PCA-aligned incidence and risk factors.
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C3-C15**: Examines the relationship between bed sharing, maternal smoking, prematurity, and SUID incidence across developmental windows.
- None of these claims directly explore hospital overnight stays or trends over time.

### Verdict

**Can this study be carried out with the given data sources?**

Yes, but with limitations:
- The design can compare proportions of SUID cases with hospital overnight stays across different periods using `ncfrp_v51` and demographic information from PRAMS datasets.
- However, caution is needed due to potential selection bias in case ascertainment over time and differences in measurement between case series and population reference data.

**Next Steps:**
- Acquire the necessary years for ncfrp_v51 (2008-2019).
- Ensure comparability of hospital stay measures by focusing on binary `INF4hosprtn` from ncfrp_v51.
- Account for potential confounding factors through robust statistical methods.
