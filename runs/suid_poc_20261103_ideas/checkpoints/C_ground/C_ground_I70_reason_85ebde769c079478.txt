### Design

**Design:** Trends within cases. Compare proportions among the cases across periods.

**Restated Question:** What is the trend in SUID incidence among infants whose mothers had pre-pregnancy health problems compared to those without, as recorded by the NFR-CRS (ncfrp_v51) from 2008-2019?

### Sources by Role

- **Case Series:** ncfrp_v51 (2008-2019)
  - **Reason:** This source records SUID cases and can provide information on maternal pre-pregnancy health conditions.
  
- **Population Reference:** prams_p7_arf, prams_p8_arf (2012-2021)
  - **Reason:** These sources provide weighted survey data to estimate the prevalence of maternal pre-pregnancy health problems in live births.

### Period

**Period Required:**
- Case Series (ncfrp_v51): 2008-2019
- Population Reference (prams_p7_arf, prams_p8_arf): 2012-2021

Since the study focuses on trends within cases over time, we need to focus primarily on the case series data from 2008-2019. The population reference data will be used for comparison purposes but does not extend as far back.

### Variables

**Maternal Pre-pregnancy Health Conditions:**
- **ncfrp_v51:** ISSprechronicdiseasebef (Chronic disease control Education - before pregnancy)
  - This variable captures information on pre-existing chronic conditions before pregnancy.
  
- **prams_p7_arf, prams_p8_arf:** PG8_SC, HTH_SC, HTH_ALRGI, HTH_ASMA, HTH_PCOS, BPG_DIAB8, BPG_HBP8, PREV_LBW_RAW, MM_FEVER
  - These variables cover various health conditions before and during pregnancy.

### Biases

**Selection Bias:**
- The case series (ncfrp_v51) only includes cases reviewed by participating fatality review teams.
- PRAMS surveys are weighted to represent live births from sampled mothers in participating states, but these sources do not include information on SUID incidence directly.

**Information Bias:**
- ncfrp_v51 captures health conditions after a child's death through medical reviews and reports; it may miss some pre-pregnancy conditions that were not documented or recorded.
- PRAMS surveys are conducted a few months postpartum, potentially missing certain conditions if they occurred prior to the survey.

**Confounding:**
- Other factors such as socioeconomic status, environmental exposures, and lifestyle behaviors (e.g., smoking) could confound the relationship between maternal pre-pregnancy health problems and SUID incidence.
  
### What the Design Cannot Establish

This design can only compare proportions among cases over time. It cannot establish causal relationships or rates due to the lack of a denominator source for calculating incidence. The study also cannot directly estimate trends in incidence across the population; it will focus on trends within the case series.

### Prior Art

**Manuscripts' Claims:**
- **SUID_manuscript_v9_clean.C10:** Excludes certain cases due to extreme preterm births, which could introduce bias if such exclusions are not accounted for.
- **bedsharing_ms_v7.C24:** Acknowledges that the case series should be understood as a growing sample rather than a fixed national census, highlighting potential changes in case ascertainment over time.

None of these claims directly address trends in SUID incidence related to maternal pre-pregnancy health conditions. The proposed study would fill this gap by focusing on such trends within the existing case data and comparing them with population reference data.

### Verdict

**Can These Sources Answer a Version of the Question?**

Yes, but with limitations:

- **Trends in cases:** The ncfrp_v51 case series can be used to analyze trends over time among SUID cases.
- **Prevalence estimates:** PRAMS surveys (prams_p7_arf and prams_p8_arf) provide weighted estimates of maternal pre-pregnancy health conditions, which can serve as a population reference.

However, the study cannot establish incidence rates or causal relationships due to the lack of a denominator source. The focus should be on comparing proportions among cases over time with corresponding trends in the population reference data.

**Logical Reasoning:**
- The question is feasible within the given sources and design.
- Comparisons between case series and population references are valid for group-level analyses but not for incidence rates or causal inference.

This study can help identify trends in SUID risk among infants of mothers with pre-pregnancy health problems over time, using available data effectively.
