# Research Design

## Working Hypothesis
More males than females die due to Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS). However, the underlying reason for this disparity could be either a higher birth rate of males or a specific predisposition of males to SIDS.

## Variables of Interest
- **DOB_TT**: Time of birth
- **MAGER9**: Mother's Age Recode 9
- **MAGER**: Mother's Age
- **SEX**: Child's Sex
- **DOD**: Date of Death
- **CAUSE**: Cause of Death
- **LOW_BIRTH_WEIGHT**: Low Birth Weight
- **BIRTH_ORDER**: Birth Order
- **PRENATAL_CARE**: Prenatal Care
- **ETHNICITY**: Ethnicity of Mother

## Research Questions

### Research Question 1
What is the distribution of male and female births over time, and does the birth rate of males significantly exceed that of females within the population under study?
- **Variables to Analyze**: 
  - `SEX`
  - `DOB_TT`
  
### Research Question 2
What is the relative incidence of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) in male and female infants, and is there a statistically significant difference in the incidence rates between the sexes?
- **Variables to Analyze**: 
  - `SEX`
  - `DOD`
  - `CAUSE`
  
### Research Question 3
How do maternal factors (age, prenatal care, and ethnic background) and infant factors (low birth weight and birth order) correlate with the incidence of SIDS, and is there a stronger correlation for males compared to females?
- **Variables to Analyze**: 
  - `MAGER`
  - `PRENATAL_CARE`
  - `ETHNICITY`
  - `LOW_BIRTH_WEIGHT`
  - `BIRTH_ORDER`
  - `SEX`
  - `CAUSE` 

## Conclusion
Through the formulation of these research questions, we aim to dissect the complexity surrounding the disparity of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome among males and females, addressing both the birth ratios and potential predispositions in males. The analysis will ultimately shed light on the causal relationships at play regarding SIDS.
