# Research Design Overview

## Working Hypothesis
**More males than females die due to Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS). But is this because more total males are born, or is it because of a male predisposition?**

## Rationale
The hypothesis aims to explore the potential gender disparity in SIDS cases, examining whether this phenomenon is fundamentally attributed to a higher birth rate of males or a specific vulnerability associated with male infants. Previous studies have indicated that males are indeed more likely to experience SIDS, and research has shown varying influences of biological and environmental factors. Understanding the underlying causes is crucial for prevention strategies and public health interventions.

## Research Questions

1. **RQ1: Is there a significant difference in the incidence rate of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) between male and female infants?**
   - **Variables:** SEX, CAUSEOFDEATH
   - **Rationale:** Previous research has documented a higher incidence of SIDS in male infants. This question seeks to quantify this difference further and establish the baseline frequency of SIDS by sex.

2. **RQ2: Do male infants have a higher birth rate compared to female infants, and does this correlate with the number of SIDS cases?**
   - **Variables:** SEX, DOB
   - **Rationale:** Understanding whether the proportion of male to female births contributes to the observed higher SIDS rates will help distinguish between sheer birth numbers and predisposition factors. Past studies have analyzed sex ratios at birth, suggesting a natural advantage of male births, which can frame current analysis.

3. **RQ3: What maternal factors (maternal age, race, and prenatal care) are associated with a higher likelihood of SIDS in male versus female infants?**
   - **Variables:** MAGER, MOTHERRACE, PRENATALCARE, SEX, CAUSEOFDEATH
   - **Rationale:** Exploring the intersection of maternal characteristics and infant gender will shed light on potential sociodemographic and prenatal influences that exacerbate SIDS risk in males. Research has linked these maternal factors to infant health outcomes, but their impact on SIDS differentiation by sex remains underexplored.

## Recommended Statistical Tests

- For **RQ1**, a **Chi-square test** can be utilized to determine if there's a significant association between the sex of the infant and the cause of death (SIDS vs other causes).
  
- For **RQ2**, **descriptive statistics** followed by **poisson regression** can be applied to analyze the relationship between birth sex ratios and incidences of SIDS, adjusting for appropriate covariates.

- For **RQ3**, a **multivariate logistic regression** could be employed to assess how various maternal characteristics relate to SIDS outcomes while controlling for infant sex.

## Speculated Interpretation of Statistical Test Results
- **RQ1**: A statistically significant difference in SIDS rates by sex would support the hypothesis that solutions should focus on gender-specific health initiatives.
  
- **RQ2**: A positive correlation between higher birth rates of males and increased SIDS cases would suggest that birth rates contribute to mortality statistics, warranting investigation into alternative preventative measures.
  
- **RQ3**: Identification of significant maternal factors associated with increased SIDS risk in male infants could indicate areas for targeted interventions and education for expecting parents and healthcare providers. 

Overall, these research questions and statistical approaches would provide a comprehensive understanding of the dynamics at play in SIDS occurrences and inform strategies for health improvement.
